ISP MANAGEMENT SYSTEM - USER MANUAL
Last updated: 2026-10-07
=================================================================

This guide explains how to use the system. There are two kinds of
accounts:

  ADMINISTRATOR  - runs the ISP: manages customers, plans, payments,
                   devices and routers.
  CUSTOMER       - a person buying internet access from the ISP.

Which one you see when you log in depends on your account. If something
on screen doesn't match this guide, the application may have changed
since this was written - check with whoever maintains it.


=================================================================
PART 1: LOGGING IN
=================================================================
Open the site and enter your Email and Password on the Sign in page,
then submit.

There is currently no "forgot password" link. If you're an
administrator and you've lost access, you'll need another administrator
to change it for you, or direct database/console access. If you're a
customer and you've lost your password, contact the ISP's administrator
- they can set you a new one from your account page.

Once logged in, an administrator lands on the admin Dashboard; a
customer lands on their own Dashboard. You can change your own password
at any time (Administrator: your profile menu / Customer: the Account
page).


=================================================================
PART 2: FOR ADMINISTRATORS
=================================================================
The left-hand menu has: Dashboard, Customers, Subscriptions, Payments,
Revenue, Devices, Unregistered Devices, Device Map, Access Points,
MikroTik Routers, Suggestions, Announcements, Notifications. Each item
has its own icon so you can find it at a glance: Dashboard (panels),
Customers (people), Subscriptions (calendar and clock), Payments (card),
Revenue (rising line), Devices (radio), Unregistered Devices (scanner),
Device Map (map pin), Access Points (mast), MikroTik Routers (router),
Suggestions (light bulb), Announcements (megaphone), Notifications
(bell).

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Dashboard
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A quick-glance summary. The tiles show:
  - Revenue (This Year)     - money received so far this calendar year
  - Revenue (This Month)
  - Devices Online          - customer devices currently online, out of
                               the total registered
  - Online APs              - Access Points currently online, out of
                               the total registered
  - Online MikroTik Routers - approved routers currently reporting in,
                               out of the total approved
  - Customer counts, plus a short list of anything needing attention
    (unregistered devices, MikroTiks awaiting approval, etc. - exact
    contents depend on what's currently pending)

Tap a tile to jump to the relevant page.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 Customers
-----------------------------------------------------------------
List of every customer. Search by name, email, phone number, company or
router MAC address, and filter by status (Active / Suspended) or type
(Residential / Business). Each row shows who they are and how to reach
them, where they are (with a landmark if one was recorded), their device
(its picture and product name), their current plan and when it ends
(or Blocked / Expired / No plan), whether their router is online, and
their account status.

Open a customer to see their detail page. On a wide screen it fills the
width, with the account and current service on the left and history,
payments and suggestions on the right; on a smaller screen it is a single
column:
  - Profile details (name, email, phone, alternate phone, address,
    landmark, and whether they are a residential or business customer -
    with the company name for businesses). "Edit" lets you change all of
    these.
  - Internal notes - a private note for administrators only. The customer
    never sees it. Edit it from the same Edit form.
  - Devices - the customer's own radio(s) with their picture, product
    name and whether they are online; click one to open it.
  - Current subscription - their active plan if they have one, or their
    most recent Blocked plan with a "Resume subscription" option
  - Subscription history - every plan they've ever had
  - Their allocated device(s), with Block / Connect / Reallocate buttons
    right there (same effect as doing it from the MikroTik page)
  - Payment history
  - "Block internet access" - cuts off whichever router device is
    allocated to them. Only appears if they have one. This doesn't
    delete their subscription, it marks it Blocked, and can be reversed
    with "Resume subscription".
  - "Suspend account" / "Reactivate account" - stops them being able to
    log in at all. This is different from blocking their internet - a
    suspended customer can't log in; a customer with blocked internet
    can still log in and see their account, just has no connection.

IMPORTANT: a customer cannot buy or be given a plan until a MikroTik
router device has been allocated to them (see 2.6, MikroTik >
Needs allocation). If you try to grant a plan to someone without one,
you'll be told so.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 Subscriptions
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Three tabs:

  Subscriptions - every subscription in the system, filterable by
    status (Active / Expired / Blocked). Shows exactly when each one
    ends and how much time is left.

  Plans - manage what's for sale. Create a plan with a Name, an
    optional Description, a Price, and how long it lasts. Duration is
    entered as a number plus a unit - Minutes, Hours or Days - so you
    can offer a 30-minute plan, a 2-hour plan, or a 30-day plan, side by
    side. Existing plans can be edited the same way.
    A plan also has a Speed:
      - Default speed - the MikroTik is told nothing about speed. If the
        customer's router currently has a speed limit (from an earlier
        plan, or one you set by hand), activating this plan removes it.
      - Set uplink and downlink limits - whenever the plan is activated
        for a customer (they buy it, you grant it, it is renewed, or you
        resume it), their router is capped at those speeds as soon as it
        confirms their connection. Uplink is
        upload (data leaving the customer), downlink is download. Type a
        number and choose kbps, Mbps or Gbps. You must give both.
    Each plan card shows its speed. Changing a plan's speed does not
    change anyone already on it straight away - the new speed is applied
    the next time the plan is activated for them. The administrators are
    notified of every plan created and every speed change, including how
    many customers are still on the old speed. To change one customer's
    speed right now, use Bandwidth Limit under MikroTik > Commands.

  Grant - give a plan to a customer directly, e.g. for a cash payment
    taken in person. Search for the customer (only customers who
    already have a router allocated will appear in this list), pick the
    plan, and confirm. This has exactly the same effect as the customer
    buying it themselves: it's recorded as a payment, and their router
    is connected automatically.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 Payments
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A record of payments received, and a form to record one manually (for
a cash payment, for example). Recording a payment against a
subscription and marking it as renewing that subscription will extend
the customer's plan and reconnect their router automatically.

Note: there is currently no live payment gateway (MTN MoMo / Orange
Money / card) wired in - every payment here is recorded directly by an
administrator, or via the customer's in-app "purchase" flow once a
gateway is configured. If a customer tries to buy a plan online before
a gateway exists, they'll see a clear message that online payment isn't
available yet.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 Revenue
-----------------------------------------------------------------
This page shows only what you choose to see - pick your options at the
top, and the chart below updates immediately:

  Period       - a quick preset (last 30 days, last 12 months, this
                 year, last year, last 5 years) or a custom date range
  Show         - Revenue, Number of payments, or Average payment
  Split by     - break the chart into separate lines/bars per Plan, or
                 per Payment method; or leave it as one overall figure
  Compare with - overlay a second line for the previous period, or the
                 same period last year
  Per          - group into days, months or years
  Chart type   - Bars, Line, Area, or Stacked bars

Below the chart, "Show numbers" reveals the exact figures behind it in
a table, and there's a CSV download if you want the raw data elsewhere.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.6 Devices
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Devices (list)     - every registered customer device (PowerBeam
                        receiver), searchable (name, MAC, serial number,
                        customer) and filterable by model, with
                        online/offline status. Each row shows the
                        device's picture and product name, its customer
                        and phone number, MAC address and serial number,
                        access point, firmware, and - while online - its
                        last signal reading.
  Device detail      - live status, signal, uptime and other metrics
                        for one device, refreshed automatically. A
                        "Console" button (top right) opens a live shell
                        session on the device, and a "Password" button
                        lets you view, generate, or set its stored admin
                        password - see below for both. The page is
                        arranged in cards:
                          Hardware - what the unit physically is:
                            product, model, product line, MAC address,
                            hostname and firmware version. "Edit" lets
                            you correct the model, MAC address and
                            firmware version. The firmware version
                            fills in by itself when the device's
                            wireless link reports it (a unit reports
                            its partner's firmware, so it appears once
                            that partner is registered too). The product name, product line
                            and picture can't be edited on their own:
                            they are worked out from the model, so
                            fixing a wrong model fixes them too.
                            "Detect from device" asks the device itself
                            (it must be online) and fills in its model,
                            hostname, IP address and network settings -
                            replacing what was stored. This also happens
                            automatically, once, for a device that is
                            missing them.
                          Network - the device's IP address, whether it
                            is static or DHCP, subnet mask, default
                            gateway, DNS servers and time zone, as the
                            device reports them. Display only. If it
                            says they haven't been read yet, use
                            "Detect from device".
                          Records - our own bookkeeping: device name,
                            record status, installation date, location
                            note, network device ID and free-text notes.
                            "Edit" changes them.
                        The device's picture is shown at the top of the
                        page and in every list. If the model isn't one
                        we have a picture for, a generic radio icon is
                        shown instead.
  Configure          - push configuration to a device (currently
                        limited to what the hardware layer supports -
                        e.g. SSID, reboot scheduling).
  History            - a chart of that device's metrics over time.
  Map                - every device plotted on a map, if location data
                        is available for it.
  Unregistered       - devices that have connected to the network but
                        aren't in the system yet, PLUS devices flagged
                        "Unknown device" - these have never contacted us
                        directly at all; one of our access points spotted
                        them sitting on its network during a scan (this
                        happens automatically every couple of minutes, or
                        click "Scan now" on an access point to check
                        immediately - see 2.7). For a normal (non-unknown)
                        entry, the system tries to work out whether it's
                        an Access Point or a Station (a customer's
                        receiver) and what it's called; if it can't tell,
                        it says so ("Role uncertain") and you can either
                        check again or decide yourself.
                          - "Register AP" files it as institution
                            infrastructure (a tower-mounted radio).
                          - "Register to a customer" links it to a
                            specific customer as their device.
                        You can rename it before registering either way.
                        Each entry shows its picture and product name.
                        When registering a customer device, the Model
                        box is filled in from what the device reported -
                        correct it if it looks wrong; it is saved on the
                        device along with its picture.
                        "Discard" removes it from the list without
                        registering it (it'll reappear if it connects
                        again later).
                        An "Unknown device" also gets an "Adopt" button -
                        see below - since it isn't under our management
                        at all yet, so there's nothing to register until
                        that succeeds and it starts reporting to us.
  Console            - a live command-line session directly on the
                        device's own shell, opened from the "Console"
                        button on its detail page. Enter the device's
                        username and password (these are the device's
                        own login credentials, not your admin account -
                        they're sent for that one session only and are
                        never saved anywhere), then type commands and
                        press Enter just like a normal terminal - use the
                        Up/Down arrow keys to recall previous commands in
                        the same session. Click "Disconnect" when done,
                        or just navigate away/close the tab - either way
                        the session is closed automatically so it doesn't
                        sit open on the device. If the device doesn't
                        respond, or the login is rejected, you'll see a
                        clear message and can try again. This only works
                        on a device that has communicated with the system
                        at least once (i.e. has a known MAC address) - a
                        freshly-registered device with no contact yet
                        won't have the Console button enabled.
  Password           - view, generate, or set the admin password stored
                        for a device (or access point - same button,
                        same behaviour, see 2.7). Unlike Console, this
                        password IS saved by the system, so you don't
                        have to remember or re-enter it each time - it's
                        stored encrypted and only an administrator can
                        view it. "Generate one for me" has the device
                        create a strong password itself; "I'll set it
                        myself" lets you type a specific one. Either way,
                        the device needs to be online right now for this
                        to work, since it's a live command to the device,
                        not just a note in our records.
  Adopt              - only on an "Unknown device" (see Unregistered
                        above) - brings a device we've only ever spotted,
                        never actually managed, under management for the
                        first time. You'll be asked for the password of
                        the access point that spotted it (skip this if
                        one is already saved for that access point - it
                        reuses that, since a brand new device typically
                        still has the same default password your access
                        points do) and a new password to set on the
                        device itself, either generated for you or typed
                        in. If it succeeds, the device should start
                        reporting to us shortly - register it as usual
                        once it does, and the password you set here will
                        be applied to it automatically. If it fails, you
                        can simply try again - the underlying adoption
                        process is still being finalized, so this isn't
                        unusual yet.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.7 Access Points
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The list of institutional Access Points (tower/rooftop radios that
serve multiple customers), separate from customer devices. Shows their
online status and location, with each one's picture and product name.
Each row also has:
  Detect model - asks the access point (it must be online) what model it
              is and saves it, which also updates its picture. This also
              happens automatically for an access point whose model is
              still unknown. Adding or editing an access point also lets
              you record its firmware version and set its status to
              Maintenance (the other statuses are kept up to date
              automatically from what the access point reports).
  Password  - same as a device's Password button (see 2.6) - view,
              generate, or set this access point's stored admin password.
  Scan now  - checks this access point's radio neighborhood right now for
              devices it can see that aren't in our system yet, rather
              than waiting for the automatic scan (which runs every
              couple of minutes on its own). Anything new shows up in
              Unregistered Devices as an "Unknown device".

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.8 MikroTik
-----------------------------------------------------------------
This page controls which customers actually have internet access,
through the MikroTik routers in the field.

  MikroTiks tab
    Every MikroTik router the system has ever heard from.
      - A brand-new router (never seen before) shows as Pending -
        Approve it once you've confirmed it's genuinely one of yours.
      - Once approved, it will keep being trusted automatically every
        time it reconnects (for example after a power cut) - you won't
        be asked to approve it again.
      - "Edit details" on a router lets you give it a friendly name,
        record where it sits (its site) and add notes. These are yours:
        the router's own reports never overwrite them. If a name is set
        it is shown instead of the name the router calls itself.
      - "Revoke" on an approved router immediately stops trusting it:
        its devices show offline, any block/connect commands waiting on
        it are marked Failed, and it will stay revoked even if it
        reconnects, until you approve it again.

  Online / Offline tabs
    Devices (customers' routers) the MikroTik network currently sees /
    doesn't currently see. An offline device is NOT deleted or
    forgotten - it stays in the system with its customer attached, so
    it picks up right where it left off when it reconnects.

  Needs allocation tab
    Devices the network can see but that aren't linked to any customer
    yet. Use "Allocate" to search for and pick the customer this device
    belongs to. Once allocated, that customer can be given a plan and
    connected.

  For any device, in any tab, you can:
    - Block  / Connect  - cut off or restore its internet access.
      This is not instant: the device will show "Pending" until the
      router confirms the change actually took effect, which normally
      takes a few seconds to under a minute.
    - Reallocate - move this device to a different customer (opens the
      same picker as Needs allocation), or Unallocate it entirely.
    - Forget - permanently remove an unallocated, offline device from
      the list. Only available when both those conditions are true. If
      it reconnects later it will simply reappear as unallocated.

  Commands tab
    This is where you send commands directly to a MikroTik. Each command
    is numbered and has a name, a short summary, and a "Read more" link
    that opens the full explanation if the summary isn't enough. Commands
    take effect on the router's next check-in, usually within about 20
    seconds.

    1. Device Access Control
       Allow a device through a MikroTik, or block it, using its MAC
       address. This works even for a device that particular MikroTik has
       never reported seeing yet. It exists mainly for devices like
       PowerBeam access points that should never be without internet: as
       soon as you know its MAC, you can pre-authorise it before it even
       joins the network, so it's never accidentally blocked the moment
       it connects.
         a. Enter the MAC address, or click one of the registered access
            points listed under "Quick pick" to fill it in automatically.
         b. Choose which MikroTik to send it to, or "All MikroTiks" to
            send it to every approved router at once.
         c. Click "Allow device" or "Block device".
       You'll see the result for each router the request was sent to. If
       a router already has an earlier command still waiting for
       confirmation on that same device, it's shown as "Already pending"
       rather than being sent again - wait for that one to resolve (check
       Command history) before sending another.

    2. Bandwidth Limit (Speed Cap)
       Limits a device's uplink (upload) and downlink (download) speed, or
       returns it to the default, without touching its internet access.
       A device with no limit is on the Default: the MikroTik is told
       nothing about its speed.
         a. Choose the device: either pick it from the list of routers
            that belong to customers (search by customer name, MAC
            address, hostname or IP), or switch to "Type a MAC
            address", enter it and choose which MikroTik it sits behind.
         b. Its current speed is shown straight away - "Default" if no
            limit was ever set, otherwise the uplink and downlink,
            whether an administrator or a plan set it, and when.
         c. Enter an uplink and a downlink and click "Set speed limit"
            (or "Change speed limit"), then confirm. To go back to the
            default, click "Remove limit (use default)".
       MikroTiks don't report speed back, so a change can't be confirmed
       from here - it shows as Queued, not Confirmed. If a block or
       connect for that device is still waiting for the router, you'll be
       asked to wait for it first. The administrators are notified of
       every speed change - who made it, which customer and device, the
       old and new speed, and whether it failed. A device's current speed
       also shows in the Devices lists.
       A plan's speed is applied automatically: when a plan is activated,
       the customer is connected first, and their speed is set as soon as
       the router confirms the connection (so it can land a short while
       after they come online). Activating a plan replaces any speed you
       set by hand.

  Command history tab
    A log of every block/connect/speed command ever sent, its status
    (Waiting for router / Confirmed / Failed, or Queued for speed
    changes), and which router it went through (shown by name). A
    block or connect that the router hasn't confirmed is sent again
    automatically, about every 30 seconds and up to 4 times in all,
    before it is given up on - "Tried 3 times" appears when that has
    happened. This covers the case where the communication service
    restarted and forgot a command it had been given. Speed
    changes show the speed that was requested, and the plan that caused
    it when a plan activation did. You can delete individual entries or clear
    the whole history; a command still waiting on a router can't be
    deleted until it resolves.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.9 Suggestions
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Feedback, complaints, compliments and support requests submitted by
customers. Open one to read it and reply; your reply is visible to the
customer who sent it.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.10 Announcements
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Write a message that gets broadcast to every customer as a
notification - for planned maintenance, outages, new plans, etc.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.11 Notifications
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Your own feed of system alerts - filterable by type (Subscription,
Payment, Announcement, Suggestion, Customer, Network, General). Notably:
  - "Network" notifications cover connection events and problems: a new
    MikroTik awaiting approval, an automatic connect/disconnect that
    happened, or one that failed and needs manual attention.
Pay attention to anything reporting a FAILED automatic action - it
means a customer's connection didn't update the way the system
intended, and needs a manual check from the MikroTik page.


=================================================================
PART 3: FOR CUSTOMERS
=================================================================
The menu has: Dashboard, Subscription, Suggestions, Notifications,
Account.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Dashboard
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Shows your current plan (if you have one), how much time is left on it
- with a live countdown - and recent notifications. If no router has
been linked to your account yet, you'll see a notice explaining that
you can't get internet access or buy a plan until an administrator sets
that up; contact them.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Subscription
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Current plan - your active plan, with a live countdown to when it
    ends and the exact time it will switch off.
  Buy a plan - browse available plans and purchase one. If you already
    have plenty of time left on your current plan, buying the same plan
    again queues it to start the moment your current one ends (so your
    time simply adds up, with no gap and no overlap). If you're running
    low, the button instead reads as a renewal.
  History - every plan you've had, when it started and ended, and its
    status (Active / Expired / Blocked).

If you see "Your router isn't linked to your account yet", you cannot
buy a plan until an administrator links one - contact them.

If your account shows as Blocked, an administrator has cut off your
internet (this is different from your plan simply expiring). Contact
them; only an administrator can lift a block.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 Suggestions
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Send feedback, a complaint, a compliment, or a support request to the
ISP. Pick a category, write a subject and message, and submit. You can
see the status of anything you've sent, and any reply from an
administrator.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 Notifications
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Everything the system or an administrator has told you, including:
  - your internet being connected or disconnected
  - reminders that your plan is about to expire (timed to how long your
    plan lasts - you'll be reminded well before a multi-day plan runs
    out, and shortly before a short plan does)
  - your router being linked to, or unlinked from, your account
  - announcements from the ISP

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.5 Account
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Your profile details, and a form to change your password. There is no
self-service "forgot password" - if you're locked out, contact the ISP.


=================================================================
PART 4: QUICK ANSWERS TO COMMON QUESTIONS
=================================================================
"A customer paid but isn't online yet"
  Check MikroTik > Command history for their device. If it shows
  "Waiting for router", give it a little time - the router hasn't
  polled yet, and the command is re-sent automatically every 30
  seconds or so for up to two minutes. If it shows "Failed", the
  message says how many times it was tried and why; check it and retry
  the connect from the customer's page or the MikroTik page. If nothing
  shows up at all, confirm a router is actually allocated to them.

"A device shows Offline but the customer says they're connected"
  The device's router may just not have reported recently - check its
  MikroTik router's own status (approved / reporting) first.

"I approved a MikroTik but it still won't send commands"
  Confirm its status is Approved, not Pending, and that it's shown as
  currently reporting (not silent). A router that has gone quiet can't
  be sent anything until it reconnects.

"Can I give someone a very short plan, like 30 minutes?"
  Yes - when creating or editing a plan, set the duration in Minutes
  instead of Days. The system disconnects the customer at the exact
  moment the plan ends, not just "sometime that day".
